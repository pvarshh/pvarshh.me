Essay 02 / Information & power

Currency

A thought about conversations, The Mentalist, and the advantage of knowing what someone else does not.

Pranav Varshney2 min read

notes to self.

We hand over a piece of paper, a card, or a tap of a phone and walk away with coffee. The exchange feels ordinary because we agree on what the currency is worth.

Information moves through our lives with less ceremony. A fact earns attention. A secret buys closeness. An opinion invites approval, or a fight. We trade constantly, even when we never name what we hope to receive.

I keep coming back to The Mentalist when I think about this economy.

The advantage

For much of the show, Red John holds the information Patrick Jane needs. He knows who Jane is, what hurts him, and where to apply pressure. Jane is chasing a name without a face, piecing together a picture someone else can already see.

A brilliant mind can still be left guessing in the dark. Jane has persistence and perception. What he lacks is access.

That imbalance makes Red John appear impossibly far ahead. Each secret Jane uncovers changes the relationship. Names, connections, the Blake Association: every discovery gives him more of the world his opponent has been operating in.

A brilliant mind can still be left guessing in the dark.

More of the picture

Jane does not become a different person each time he learns something. His ability was already there. The information gives it somewhere to go.

This is what interests me about their rivalry. We are quick to call someone brilliant when they seem to anticipate everything. We are slower to ask what they know that everyone else does not.

Intelligence matters. So does the picture it has to work with. Before I mistake someone’s advantage for an unreachable gift, I want to know how much of the room they can see.
